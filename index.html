Health systems, CCOs and providers

Pilot new technology without carrying the whole burden of it.

Our flagship program pairs Oregon care organizations with vetted health technology companies for structured, time bound pilots. Rainshadow supplies the coordination, the evaluation design and the shared learning, so a clinic with four staff does not have to build an innovation function to take part.

Why Oregon needs this and does not have it.

Oregon has the institutions, not the table

A first rank academic health centre, a state rural health office with real data, more than a decade of coordinated care organizations and a serious technology industry. None of that is a place where a rural chief executive can see what is coming and say whether it would work in their county.

Every pilot starts from zero

A company that wants to test in Oregon finds the same closed doors in a different order each time. A clinic that wants to try something has to run its own diligence, write its own agreement and carry its own risk. Both sides spend the budget on the setup and never reach the result.

The money arrived before the capacity did

Federal rural health money is moving now and the period of performance ends. Organisations without an innovation function will spend it on what they already know how to buy. That is a reasonable thing to do and it is how a once in a generation award produces nothing new.

New Mexico has shown the shape of the answer

Oregon is not first. New Mexico stood up a statewide convener, named priorities out of its own health systems and put state money behind a pilot network. Rainshadow is built on that model deliberately, with Oregon's coordinated care structure and its nine tribal nations in the design from the start. See HealthInno.
